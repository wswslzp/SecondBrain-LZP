## 4\. The neuroscience of why your brain scrolls past 99% of content4. 你的大脑为什么会忽略 99%内容的神经营学原理

Your brain runs on glucose. Literal sugar. Every time you think, focus, or process information, your brain is burning fuel to do it.你的大脑以葡萄糖为燃料。字面意义上的糖。每次你思考、专注或处理信息时，你的大脑都在燃烧燃料来完成这些。

**And your brain hates burning fuel.而你的大脑讨厌燃烧燃料。**

It's lazy by design. It evolved over millions of years to conserve energy whenever possible, because in nature, energy was scarce. If you wasted too much energy thinking about things that didn't matter, you died.它天生就懒惰。它经过数百万年的演化，尽可能节省能量，因为在自然界中，能量是稀缺的。如果你浪费太多能量去思考无关紧要的事情，你就会死掉。

So your brain developed a ruthless filter: only spend glucose on things that are either survival-relevant or impossibly easy to process. Everything else gets ignored before you're even aware of it.所以你的大脑形成了一个无情的过滤器：只将葡萄糖花费在那些要么与生存相关、要么极易处理的事情上。其他一切都在你意识到之前就被忽略了。

That filter is still running today. Except now, instead of filtering out which berry to eat, it's filtering out which post to stop on while you're scrolling Instagram.那个过滤器今天仍在运行。只是现在，它不再是筛选吃哪些浆果，而是筛选你在刷 Instagram 时该停留在哪些帖子上。

And this is where it gets wild.而这里就开始疯狂了。

Your brain makes the decision to scroll or stop before you've consciously seen the content.你的大脑在你有意识地看到内容之前，就已经决定是滚动还是停止。

By the time "you" look at a post, your brain has already decided whether it's worth processing. If the visual looks complicated, if the text is dense, if the message isn't instantly clear - your brain flags it as "expensive to process" and your thumb moves before you even know why.当“你”看到一篇帖子时，你的大脑已经决定了它是否值得处理。如果视觉效果看起来复杂，如果文本过于密集，如果信息不是瞬间清晰——你的大脑会将其标记为“expensive to process”，你的拇指就会在你还没意识到为什么之前就滑动过去了。

You're not choosing what to watch. Your brain is choosing for you, based on how much glucose it would cost to understand it. And it's choosing "next" 99% of the time.你并不是在选择看什么。你的脑子在为你选择，基于理解它需要消耗多少葡萄糖。而且它 99%的时间都在选择“下一个”。

There was a study my team and I recently looked at that broke this down visually. It measured what people actually see as a post enters their field of view during scrolling. 我们团队最近看过的一项研究，用视觉方式将这一点分解开来。它测量了人们在滚动时，当帖子进入他们的视野范围时，实际看到了什么。

![Image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HGBMUiEWAAEwkEr?format=jpg&name=large)

The finding: the brain is making scroll-or-stop decisions based on just the top portion of a post, before the full thing has even fully entered the screen.发现：大脑仅基于帖子的顶部部分，就在整个帖子甚至完全进入屏幕之前做出滚动或停止的决定。

If that top portion doesn't immediately register as easy to process, the decision is already made. You scroll. The post is dead before you've read a word of it.如果顶部那部分没有立即被视为易于处理，决定就已经做出了。你滑动滚动了。在你读一个字之前，这篇帖子就已经死了。

This is why headlines and visual hierarchy in your ads matters so much. It's why the hook of your video has to land in the first half-second (and why you need a hook text on your video ads).这就是为什么你的广告中的标题和视觉层次如此重要。这就是为什么你的视频钩子必须在头半秒内抓住观众（以及为什么你的视频广告需要钩子文字）。

It's why text-heavy ads get crushed by simple, visual ones. It's not about attention spans getting shorter. It's about the brain making survival-era decisions on every single piece of content you've ever seen.这就是为什么文字密集的广告会被简单、直观的视觉广告碾压。这不是因为注意力持续时间变短。而是因为大脑对每一件你所见过的内容都在做出生存时代式的决策。

And now apply this to AI animation ads.现在将此应用到 AI 动画广告上。

Think about what those ads actually look like. Simple shapes. Clear characters. Obvious problems and obvious solutions. A voice narrating in a cadence that's nearly impossible to misinterpret. Everything about them is engineered (accidentally or not) to be stupidly, almost embarrassingly easy for the brain to process.想想那些广告实际上长什么样。简单的形状。清晰的字符。显而易见的问题和显而易见的解决方案。一个声音以几乎不可能被误解的节奏叙述。它们的一切都是（无论有意还是无意）设计成愚蠢地、几乎尴尬地容易被大脑处理。

Your brain sees an animated ingredient with a face explaining what it does, and instead of flagging it as "expensive to process," it flags it as "basically a cartoon - zero work to understand." Your brain relaxes. Your defenses drop. And now whatever message is embedded in that cartoon gets through.你的脑子看到一个长着脸的动画食材在解释它的作用，而不是将其标记为“处理成本高”，而是将它标记为“基本上是个卡通——零理解成本”。你的脑子放松了。你的防御降低了。现在，卡通中嵌入的任何信息都能通过。

Every other format on the internet is competing for attention. AI animation ads aren't competing for attention. They're slipping past the filter entirely.互联网上的其他所有格式都在争夺注意力。AI 动画广告并不在争夺注意力。它们完全绕过了过滤器。

That's why they work. That's why governments use them. That's why we spent $100k last 7 days on 1 single ad. Not because the content is better. Because the brain doesn't have to work to process it.这就是它们奏效的原因。这就是政府使用它们的原因。这就是我们过去 7 天在单个广告上花费$100k 的原因。不是因为内容更好。因为大脑不需要费力去处理它。

## 5a. What Tribe V2 actually is5a. Tribe V2 实际上是什么

Tribe V2 is an AI model that simulates how the human brain reacts to video.Tribe V2 是一个 AI 模型，它模拟人类大脑对视频的反应方式。

Not "predicts engagement." Not "estimates watch time." **It actually simulates, second by second, what's happening inside a human brain as it consumes a piece of content.**不是“predicts engagement”。不是“estimates watch time”。它真正逐秒模拟人类大脑在消费一段内容时内部发生的一切。

It's not an engagement predictor dressed up in fancy language. It's a synthetic viewer. One that has a brain. One that reacts to your content the way a real human would, before a single real human ever sees it.这不是用华丽语言包装的互动预测器。这是一个合成观众。一个有大脑的观众。一个会在任何真人看到之前，像真人一样对你的内容做出反应的观众。

## 5b. How Meta built this5b. Meta 是如何构建这个的

Meta took 720 real humans. They wired them to MRI scanners. They had them consume thousands of hours of content - social media posts, movies, podcasts, ads, video clips of every possible format. And while those people were watching, the MRI scanners were capturing what was happening inside their brains in real time.Meta 找来了 720 名真人。他们将这些人连接到 MRI 扫描仪上。让他们消费了数千小时的内容——社交媒体帖子、电影、播客、广告、各种格式的视频片段。而在这些人观看时，MRI 扫描仪实时捕捉他们大脑内部发生的一切。

That's the training data.这就是训练数据。

Then Meta fed all of that data into an AI model and trained it to predict, based on a piece of content, what the brain would do when it saw it.然后 Meta 将所有这些数据输入到一个 AI 模型中，并训练它根据一段内容预测大脑在看到它时会做什么。

That's Tribe V2. It's the compressed neural reaction pattern of 720 people who lived inside an MRI machine for a combined lifetime of hours, rolled into a model anyone with an internet connection can now run.这就是 Tribe V2。它是 720 个人在 MRI 机器内总共度过数小时后的压缩神经反应模式，被浓缩成一个现在任何有互联网连接的人都能运行的模型。
